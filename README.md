Ohrwurm is a product design semester project done at University of the Arts in Berlin in 2025. Dealing with the topic of overstimulation through everyday unavoidable city noises I developed this reactive audio patch. 

Instead of shutting out disturbing noises the the patcher alters them to be easier for the listener to endure. The Filter consit of two components. The first one is the 'construction detection'. The surrounding noises are detected through different filters (lowpass, highpass, etc.). With the help of a given threshoold, certain sound snippets are recorded. These snippets are used in the second component called drumnoise maschine. Here the user can create a beat pattern with a generative drum maschine. Using the noises surrounding noises the beat changes when the noise changes.

Theres another filter implemented in the patch called pitch shift. You can manually give it a frequency. Once that frequency is then detected by the 'construction detection' the tone is altered to its correspoinding chords to form a major chord. But I didn't further develop this filter. 

The audio filters coded in MAX MSP relie on a research about psychoacoustics, especially the topic of rythmic masking. 

The filters were working on a walkman I 3D printed and soldered.
